General Information

An Adult Family Home is a residential house in a regular neighbourhood where a small number of adults live and receive care and supervision. It is licensed by the state and is often described as a smaller, more personal alternative to a large assisted living facility.

Scale is the main difference. Assisted living facilities typically house dozens or hundreds of residents. An Adult Family Home houses a handful, in a real house, usually with the same small team of caregivers every day.

Care Services

Typically help with daily activities such as bathing, dressing and mobility, medication support, meals, housekeeping, laundry, companionship and supervision. The exact list depends on each home's licensed scope of care and the resident's assessment.

Some Adult Family Homes offer memory support; others do not. This depends on the home's licensed scope and staffing. Please ask directly and confirm before assuming.

Through a conversation and assessment with the family and, where appropriate, health professionals. The purpose is to check whether the home can meet the person's needs within its licensed scope of care.

Safety and Supervision

Staffing arrangements vary between homes. Some have awake overnight staff, others have on-call arrangements. Ask specifically about night coverage and get it in writing.

Typical features include grab bars, non-slip surfaces, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, clear pathways and secure entry points. Confirm the actual features during your tour.

Adult Family Homes in Washington are licensed and monitored by the state Department of Social and Health Services. Ask the home for its licence number and check the state's public records.

Costs and Payments

Rates vary considerably by home, location and level of care. This prototype deliberately does not list prices. Ask each home directly for its current rates and what they include.

Some Adult Family Homes accept state-funded programs for eligible residents. Availability varies. Ask each home whether it participates and speak with a benefits advisor about your situation.

Possible extras include personal supplies, transport, or higher care levels. Ask for a written breakdown of what the base rate covers before signing anything.
