Admission Process

A clear, unhurried path from first conversation to move-in day — with no obligation at any stage.

Who May Benefit

Is an Adult Family Home the right fit?

Adult Family Homes suit people who would benefit from supervision, personal care and company in a small residential setting — often those for whom a large assisted living facility feels overwhelming or impersonal.

An Adult Family Home may be a good fit if your loved one:

  • Needs help with daily activities but values privacy
  • Prefers a quiet, home-like environment
  • Would benefit from consistent caregivers
  • Has family who want to stay closely involved
  • Enjoys company and shared meals
  • Would find a large facility disorienting or stressful

Whether the home can meet a specific person's needs depends on an individual assessment and the home's licensed scope of care. Nothing on this page guarantees admission.

Step by Step

How admission works

Timing varies for every family. There's no deadline and no pressure.

Initial family consultation

A conversation by phone or in person to understand the situation and answer your first questions. Usually 20–30 minutes. No obligation, no paperwork.

Care needs assessment

We review the level of support required and whether it falls within the home's licensed scope of care. This may involve talking with health professionals (with consent).

Home visit and tour

See the home, meet the team and ask anything you like — including about licensing, staffing, meals, safety features and typical days.

Care plan discussion

Together we agree what care will look like day to day, how often it will be reviewed, and how changes will be communicated to the family.

Documentation and agreement

Required paperwork and the residency agreement are completed with the administrator. This includes disclosure of rights, policies and procedures.

Move-in preparation

We agree a move-in date and help make the room feel like the resident's own. We can discuss what to bring, what to leave behind, and how to make the first day as calm as possible.

Ongoing family communication

Regular updates and scheduled reviews keep families informed as needs change. We agree the rhythm with each family at move-in.

Preparing for Move-In

What to bring, and what to leave

Bring

  • Clothing suited to the season and personal style
  • Comfortable, safe footwear
  • Toiletries and personal care items
  • Photos, familiar objects, a favourite blanket
  • Any mobility aids (walker, cane, wheelchair)
  • List of current medications and prescribing doctors
  • Insurance cards and identification documents

Leave behind or discuss first

  • Valuables and large amounts of cash
  • Furniture that won't fit or isn't needed
  • Medications not on the prescribing list
  • Items that could be trip hazards
  • Pets (discuss first — arrangements vary)
  • Anything with a strong scent, in shared rooms

This list is a general guide. The home will provide its own checklist during the admission conversation.
